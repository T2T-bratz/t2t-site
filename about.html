I ran a company to seven figures a year and then watched it fail. It was a crash course, and it cost more than money. I've made AI-driven workforce decisions at real scale. And I spent five years as the person who thought he needed to know all the answers, which mostly shut down better ideas from people who actually were experts.

What I keep coming back to is systems over heroics. If the bathroom is dirty, the question isn't who grabs the cloth. It's why the person whose job it is was too busy to clean it. Fix that and you don't have a clean bathroom, you have a consistently clean bathroom. Most of what I write is some version of that.

By day I work in HR consulting and recruitment with small and mid-sized businesses. This site isn't that. Nothing here is a pitch. It's the stuff I'd tell you over a coffee.

A few things I keep landing on. You don't need to know all the answers, and trying to is expensive. Hire for attitude and train for skill. Don't start by searching for AI tools; it's an endless black hole. Start with a clear problem statement and work backwards. And personal growth and professional growth are separate things that have to operate in harmony, or you pay for it in friction and burnout.

I run most of my own week on systems I built, including a small crowd of AI agents that handle the boring parts. I write about what actually holds up, not what sounds good.

If any of that is useful to you, the monthly note is where it lands first.
